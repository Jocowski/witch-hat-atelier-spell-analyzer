# Sealchair Glyph

> An extremely complex niche spell that drives the autonomous movement of the Sealchair — a magically-powered mobility chair used by witches who cannot walk.

## Overview
The Sealchair Glyph is the spell that animates the Sealchair, a legged contraption functioning like a wheelchair. It is used by Beldaruit and Custas, who are both unable to walk. The glyph was glimpsed when Custas's chair was knocked over by thieves (ch. 43, page 1), but the signs composing it are too small to identify in the published panels. The wiki describes it as "incredibly complex," consisting of a **main spell with 12 satellite spells surrounding it** — four large and eight small — making it one of the most complex canon contraptions depicted.

## In plain terms (sum up)
This is the magical engine inside a walking wheelchair. It is too complex to read from the available manga art, but functionally it makes a multi-legged chair walk, navigate, and respond to the rider's will. Think of it as a magical autopilot for a mechanical device. Beyond seeing that it is a multi-circle contraption (a central spell surrounded by 12 sub-seals), we can only theorize about its individual components.

## Deduced effect
The Sealchair Glyph animates and drives the legged Sealchair. Based on the described function and the one tentative sign identification, the following **ranked hypotheses** are offered:

**Hypothesis 1 (most likely): Dancing Puppet controls the legs.**
The Puppet sign (dancing_puppet) in the Flying Puppet of Diversion spell causes objects to "dart about." Applied to the chair's legs through the Sealchair's mechanisms, one or more Puppet-based sub-seals could provide the coordinated stepping motion. A wind or air sigil core would supply the substance for the Puppet's animating effect (consistent with the Flying Puppet of Diversion's wind-based composition). *What would confirm it:* a clear view of the central sign, or a wind-family sigil at the main spell's center.

**Hypothesis 2 (possible): Multiple specialized sub-seals for each leg.**
The 4 large + 8 small satellite structure may correspond to 4 legs (each with a large coordinator sub-seal) plus 8 smaller modifier seals (fine-tuning gait, balance, or turning). The main central spell would coordinate them. *What would confirm it:* a structural map showing each satellite's position relative to the chair's leg mechanisms.

**Hypothesis 3 (long-shot): A navigation/intent-reading layer.**
Given canon's established caster-intent principle (a seal responds to the caster's will within the slack of its symbols), the main spell might include a Puppet or Sights Set component that reads the rider's directional intent. *What would confirm it:* any documentation of how the rider steers (manual controls, or pure mental direction).

All three hypotheses may be simultaneously true in different parts of the 13-circle contraption.

## Element behavior & canon grounding
The Sealchair Glyph is the most under-documented complex contraption in the known spell catalog. The only canon observation about its composition is:
1. It is "incredibly complex."
2. It has a main spell with 12 surrounding satellite spells (4 large, 8 small).
3. One sign **possibly** resembles the Puppet (dancing_puppet) keystone.

From CORE.md §5 (multi-circle contraptions): satellite spells (linked seals) "combine" along their relations — a nested inner seal is wrapped by its outer; linked seals combine or stack power; an empty enclosing ring is a boundary/chamber; a coreless ring with signs is a modifier. The 12 satellites here are the most complex linked structure in the corpus.

**Closest canon analogue — Flying Puppet of Diversion.** If the Puppet sign is confirmed, the Flying Puppet of Diversion (wind + dancing_puppet signs, which sends objects darting through the air) is the closest relative. However, the Sealchair Glyph acts on a *physical object's legs* (a manipulate problem), not a free-floating magical construct, which suggests additional signs or a different sigil family handling the physical contact.

**Bottom line.** The Sealchair Glyph is a documented but unreadable contraption. Its existence and function are canon-confirmed; its composition is not. Any recipe here would be invention, not documentation. Mark as DOCS-ONLY — no catalog entry warranted.
