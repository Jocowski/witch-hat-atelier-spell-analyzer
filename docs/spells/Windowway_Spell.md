# Windowway Spell

> A paired spatial-link spell that opens a gateway between two distant locations; objects and people passing through one gateway emerge from the other.

## Overview
The Windowway Spell (JP: 扉窓の魔法, *Tobiramado no mahō*) is one of the most prominent spells in Witch Hat Atelier, appearing from chapter 2 onward as the primary means of long-distance travel for witches. It creates a persistent portal between two paired locations. The spell is subject to high variability — each instance is unique to prevent accidental cross-linking — and **cannot function without a matching pair** at the destination.

## In plain terms (sum up)
A Windowway is a door between two places that can be far apart. Draw the same spell twice (one at each end), and the two drawings link up to become a two-way passage: step through one and you come out the other. Witches use these as their network of long-distance travel routes — Qifrey's atelier, the Great Hall, and distant sites all connect through them. Each windowway is drawn slightly differently so it only links to its exact pair, not to other windowways. They cannot be used alone; you need both doors.

## Validity
**Valid — pair required.** The spell itself is well-structured (bolt ring + direction signs), but it is a portal spell that fundamentally requires a **matched pair** to function — a single drawn Windowway cannot open without a counterpart at the other end. A solitary instance is a prepared door with no destination.

## Composition
- **Substance (sigils):** No elemental sigil is identified in the wiki description. The spell operates on **space** rather than a classical element — it may use an unidentified spatial sigil, or the door/symbol at the center (described as corresponding to the destination location) may serve a sigil-like function. The center symbol is noted as potentially being "simply an indicator," so it may not be part of the spell's mechanics.
- **Form (signs):**
  - **Bolt (outer ring)** — lightning-bolt-shaped signs around the outer band. As a `form` operator, Bolt fragments the output into fast-flying pieces ([signs.md](../signs.md)). In a spatial context, "bolt" shapes would drive the piercing, penetrating quality of the portal itself — the gateway punches through space rather than leaking or pooling.
  - **Region / direction signs (inner ring, facing opposite to Bolt)** — described as "similar to directional, pointing in the opposite direction" from the outer ring. These are most likely **Region (id: `direction`)** signs facing inward or in the opposed pairing described for Floating Drops ("magic emerges only along the ring between them"). This creates a contained channel: the bolt-form shapes the portal as a pass-through conduit, and the opposed Region signs confine the spatial rift to a defined opening.
- **Location marker at center:** described as "a symbol which seems to correspond to the location the windowway links up with." Likely an identifying glyph unique to each pair, not a functional sign.
- **Ring:** closed (active).
- **Ink / dyes:** plain conjuring ink (not specified otherwise).

## Deduced effect
The **Bolt** ring provides the form: a piercing, fast-fragmenting channel that "punches" a gap through space. The **Region signs facing the opposing direction** confine this to a defined opening zone (the "along the ring between them" mechanic). Together they create a stable spatial aperture — a door-shaped rift held open by the balanced inward/outward push of the two sign-rings. The **pair requirement** means the same construction at both ends creates two mutually-attracted apertures that resolve into a single passage.

> **Note on incomplete identification:** The "lightning bolt shaped signs" and "similar to directional" inner signs are the wiki's visual description, not confirmed sign ids. The Bolt/Region mapping is the closest match in the current palette (bolt = lightning-shaped form; region = chevron/directional). This mapping should be treated as **medium confidence** until canon art is examined directly.

## Element behavior & canon grounding
The Windowway Spell is categorized as a **space-type** spell — distinct from the four classical elements (fire, water, earth, wind) and from the light/vision/time families. As with Memory Erasure and other "abstract domain" spells, it operates on a non-physical substrate. No elemental sigil is confirmed, which places this outside the standard CORE.md substance model: there is no "water" or "fire" to reason about capability/state/source from.

The **Bolt** sign's `form` operator normally fragments a substance into fast bolts. In a spatial spell, "fragmenting space" reads as creating a sharp aperture — not a blob or a cloud but a defined, penetrating gap, which matches how windowways look and behave (a crisp doorway, not a foggy dissolve). The **Region** opposition (opposed pairs → "magic emerges only along the ring between them," signs.md:96) then pins this aperture to the ring band itself — the gateway is exactly at the ring boundary, not inside or outside it. This is the most physically coherent reading of both signs in a spatial context.

The **pair requirement** is the most unusual aspect. Most spells are self-contained (one seal = one effect). A Windowway requires a linked counterpart, making it a **two-point contraption** spread across space. The variability built into each pair ("each windowway is unique so as to avoid linking to the same place") mirrors how in-world witches must individually commission and calibrate each portal rather than using a single universal stamp.

**Closest canon analogues:**
- **Windowway ring in the Cloak Spell** — Sasaran incorporates a Windowway-type ring around his cloak's main spell body, using the same architecture to make the cloak a spatial pass-through point.
- **Floating Drops** — the only other canon spell that explicitly uses the "opposed Region pairs → magic along the ring" mechanic (signs.md:96); the Windowway may use the same geometry to define its aperture.

**Bottom line:** The Windowway Spell is a spatial portal built from a Bolt-shaped outer ring (gives the aperture its penetrating form) and opposed Region signs on the inner ring (confines the rift to a precise opening). The pair requirement is intrinsic — a single Windowway is a prepared door with no destination. The sign identification is medium-confidence; the behavior is canon-confirmed from dozens of appearances.

## How to draw it
1. Draw any identifying **center symbol** corresponding to the destination (this may be non-functional; it distinguishes paired windowways from each other).
2. Surround it with an **inner ring of Region (direction) signs** facing *away* from the center — pointed outward, or in the opposed arrangement.
3. Surround that with an **outer ring of Bolt signs** pointing inward (toward the ring band).
4. Close the ring.
5. Draw a **matching, slightly different version** at the destination location — the pair difference prevents cross-linking to other windowways.
6. Maintain radial symmetry for stability.

## Usage ideas
- **Long-distance transport network:** the primary WHA use — a portal network connecting the Great Hall, ateliers, and test sites.
- **Emergency evacuation:** send people to safety through a pre-drawn windowway (ch. 27: Euini and Alaira sent to safety through a windowway).
- **Remote site access:** reach otherwise inaccessible or dangerous locations without physical travel.
- **Combat portal:** incorporated into Sasaran's Cloak Spell to allow spells to be cast through the cloak from a different location.

## Similar spells
- **Cloak Spell** — contains a Windowway ring as one of its three sub-spell components.
- **Floating Drops** — the only other canon spell confirmed to use opposed Region signs to constrain magic to the ring boundary.

## Notes & limitations
- **Pair required:** a single Windowway cannot open; it needs a matching counterpart at the destination. This is a hard functional constraint, not just a design note.
- **Each windowway is unique:** they are individually calibrated to avoid cross-linking. Mass-producing identical stamps would cause portal confusion.
- Sign identification (Bolt/Region) is **medium confidence** — based on the wiki's visual descriptions ("lightning bolt-shaped" / "similar to directional, pointing opposite direction"). Confirmed sign ids would require examination of canon art.
- The center location symbol's status as a functional vs. non-functional mark is explicitly unresolved by the wiki.
- Not forbidden: spatial linking is permitted magic; it does not affect bodies or violate the forbidden categories.
