# Time Stop

> Freezes an object completely in time — total temporal stasis for as long as the seal holds — with no sign-based scoping, unlike the Warmth-Retention Seal.

## Overview
Time Stop is a time-type spell built around the **Stop sigil** (`stop`) that produces complete temporal stasis on the target. Where the **Warmth-Retention Seal** pairs Stop with a Fire sigil to freeze only the heat-aspect of an object, and where **Petrification** is a complex forbidden multi-sign seal, Time Stop appears to be the simplest form of the Stop sigil's power: a spell that outright halts time for whatever it affects, with no narrowing sigil to scope the halt to one property. The spell has no dedicated wiki page (the link was a red-link/404 as of the date of this document); this doc reasons from the Stop sigil description in sigils.md and from the Petrification/Warmth-Retention comparators.

## In plain terms (sum up)
The Stop sigil, used in its minimal form, freezes the target completely — time halts for it. Whatever state it is in when the seal activates is the state it stays in, indefinitely, until the seal is removed or fades. Unlike Counterclock (which reverses time temporarily) or Repetition (which continuously resets), Time Stop simply *pauses*. A flame put under Time Stop neither grows nor dies out; water neither flows nor evaporates; a falling object hangs in mid-air. The exact construction of Time Stop — what signs, if any, surround the Stop sigil — is unknown because no recipe source exists. This document records ranked hypotheses.

## Deduced effect
**Deduced reasoning — total temporal stasis.** The Stop sigil without a scoping partner sigil produces a total halt of the target's timeline. Per sigils.md:107: "outright halts time for the objects it affects." This differs from the Warmth-Retention mechanism (which pairs Stop + Fire to scope the halt to heat only) — Time Stop is the unscoped Stop, therefore the halt is *total* rather than property-specific.

Because no signs are documented, the questions are: (1) can Time Stop run sign-free like Warmth-Retention Seal, (2) does it use signs to project the effect, and (3) how does it compare to Petrification?

**Ranked hypotheses for the composition:**

1. **Most likely — sign-free Stop sigil (minimal seal):** The simplest Time Stop is a Stop sigil alone (or with a ring but no signs), exactly like Warmth-Retention Seal but without the Fire scoping partner. No form signs because the halt is total — there is no substance being projected, only a suppression of change. This is consistent with the pattern: Warmth-Retention (Stop + Fire, no signs = scoped halt) implies the unscoped version is simply Stop alone, no signs. *Effect:* total stasis on whatever the seal touches. *Confirmation:* any canon image of a Time Stop seal showing only the Stop sigil with no ring signs.

2. **Possible — Stop sigil + Column signs (directed stasis):** A Stop sigil ringed by Column signs would project the halt-field outward as a beam or column, freezing targets at a distance rather than requiring contact. This would make Time Stop a ranged weapon — freeze objects mid-flight, halt an enemy's motion from afar. *Effect:* a directed stasis beam. *Confirmation:* canon image showing Column signs around a Stop sigil; or a use case where the caster freezes something without physical contact with the seal.

3. **Possible — Stop sigil + Convergence signs (focused compact stasis field):** Convergence would contract the stasis effect to a precise focal point, making Time Stop a scalpel rather than a blanket — freeze one object in a crowd without freezing its neighbors. *Effect:* targeted point-stasis. *Confirmation:* canon image showing Convergence around a Stop sigil.

4. **Long-shot — Stop sigil + Region signs (zoned stasis):** Region signs would establish a spatial zone where time halts for everything inside. This would explain how a Time Stop can freeze large volumes (a room, a battlefield zone). *Effect:* area stasis. *Confirmation:* canon image showing Region signs around a Stop sigil, or a use that freezes multiple objects simultaneously.

## Element behavior & canon grounding
Stop's substance is **time** (immaterial, acts on the target's state-history). Its documented behavior: "outright halts time for the objects it affects" and "by pairing it with other sigils, it becomes possible to stop time for specific aspects of an object, such as changes in heat if paired with a fire sigil" (sigils.md:107). The second sentence is the scoping rule; the first sentence is the unscoped baseline.

The three Stop-based spells listed in sigils.md are:
- **Petrification** — a complex, multi-sign, multi-sigil forbidden seal. The "total stasis" aspect of Petrification is plausibly the Stop sigil's contribution; the rest of that seal's complexity drives other effects.
- **Warmth-Retention Seal** — Stop + Fire, no signs. The textbook scoping example.
- **Time Stop** — no recipe source. By elimination from the other two, Time Stop is the *unscoped* Stop (no second sigil) in a deployment-focused configuration (some signs to direct or contain the halt).

The key distinction from Petrification: Petrification is documented as forbidden and complex, suggesting it goes beyond simple stasis (perhaps it also transforms the target into stone, or permanently alters its nature). Time Stop, being listed separately and without a forbidden flag, is presumably a legal spell that provides stasis without the additional dangerous modification.

**Closest canon analogues:**
- **Warmth-Retention Seal** — same Stop core, scoped halt; the narrowed sibling. Time Stop is the unscoped version.
- **Petrification** — a complex forbidden version of Stop-based magic with additional effects.
- **Counterclock** — a contrasting time operation (reversal vs. halt); no sigil overlap.

**Bottom line:** Time Stop is almost certainly the minimal unscoped form of the Stop sigil — a total temporal stasis applied to whatever it touches or is aimed at. The absence of a recipe source prevents any catalog entry; this document records only the reasoning from first principles and ranked hypotheses. The most likely composition is sign-free (like Warmth-Retention) or with basic Column/Convergence signs for reach. It is not forbidden on its own (unlike drawing Counterclock on a human body), though freezing a living person would raise ethical issues under the spirit of the magic laws.
