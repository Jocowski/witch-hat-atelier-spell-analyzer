# Slime Rendering Seal

> A forbidden body-affecting spell that reportedly transforms or renders a person's body into a slime-like state; one of the few spells confirmed to use glaive signs, which control how deeply magic penetrates living flesh.

## Overview
The Slime Rendering Seal (also called the Slime Transformation Spell in some sources) is listed among the forbidden spells in [forbidden-magic.md](../forbidden-magic.md) and appears as "Slime Rendering Seal" in the wiki's navigation under Forbidden Seals. The dedicated wiki page is a red-link with no content (404). The only substantive canonical detail comes from the Signs Explained wiki page: glaive signs, which are documented as controlling how deeply magic embeds in living flesh, have "only ever been seen in the memory erasure spell variants and the slime rendering seal drawn by Engendale." This confirms:
1. Engendale (a Brimmed Cap) is the known caster.
2. The spell uses **glaives** as a key component.
3. The output is described as rendering the body into something slime-like — a physical body transformation, which is the definition of forbidden magic.

## In plain terms (sum up)
The Slime Rendering Seal is a forbidden transformation spell that turns a person's body into a slime-like substance. Like the Scalewolf Curse, it directly transforms the body — the exact category of magic banned by the Day of the Pact. What is unique about it is its use of glaive signs, which are the same pre-Pact signs found in the Memory Erasure spell. In that spell, glaives control how deeply magic reaches into flesh to erase memories. In the Slime Rendering Seal, those same glaives presumably drive the transformative magic into the body to reconstitute it as a slime. The full recipe is unknown; only the glaive component and the general effect have been documented.

## Deduced effect
**Confirmed:** the spell transforms a person into a slime-like form. The glaives guarantee the magic physically embeds in the target's flesh to produce this effect.

Ranked hypotheses for the mechanism:

1. **Most likely — glaives + water-family sigil for body liquefaction:** A water sigil (or fluid-type sigil) paired with glaives could cause the body's composition to shift toward a liquid/viscous state. The glaives ensure the water-element magic penetrates the cellular level, reconstituting solid tissue as a fluid slime. This would be analogous to the Petrification spell's "earth converts all matter to stone" but in reverse — fluid instead of rigid.

2. **Possible — glaives + unknown pre-Pact body-substance sigil:** The spell may use a specialized sigil that has no equivalent in the post-Pact standard vocabulary — a pre-Pact "flesh substance" sigil that directly manipulates biological matter. The glaives would then be the depth-regulators for how thoroughly the transformation proceeds.

3. **Possible — glaives acting as the primary agents + a fluidity-inducing sign:** Rather than relying on a particular sigil substance, the glaives (driven deeply enough) could themselves produce the slime effect by mechanically disrupting the structural integrity of tissues, with the spell's sign set then introducing a fluidity sign (like weave, billowing, or an unknown sign) to create the characteristic slime consistency.

4. **Long-shot — related to the Scalewolf Curse mechanic:** If the Scalewolf Curse works by imposing a creature-identity on the body, the Slime Rendering Seal might impose a non-creature substance-identity (slime/fluid) on the body by similar means, but with glaives substituting for whatever sign structure the Scalewolf Curse uses to interface with the body. The two spells would be mechanistic siblings using different tools for the same class of forbidden body-transformation.

*All hypotheses are speculative; the full composition is unknown.*

## Element behavior & canon grounding
Glaives are a pre-Pact sign type with a unique role: they control depth of magical penetration into living tissue. This makes them the fundamental tool for any spell that must act *inside* a body rather than on its surface. The Memory Erasure (the only other confirmed glaive spell) uses them to reach into the mind. The Slime Rendering Seal uses them to reach into the flesh and convert it.

The fact that glaives appear in only two confirmed spells — one sanctioned (Memory Erasure), one forbidden (Slime Rendering Seal) — suggests they were a common pre-Pact tool for body-magic generally, and the Day of the Pact's prohibition forced them out of use except where specially permitted. Engendale's use of the Slime Rendering Seal is therefore a direct use of pre-Pact forbidden art.

No substance in the standard documented system (fire, water, earth, wind, light, crystal, time) has "slime" as a natural output. This strongly implies either an unlisted substance sigil (pre-Pact body-matter sigil) or that the transformation works by reconstituting the body's existing material (water and other biological fluids) through glaive-driven manipulation — similar in concept to how earth can reconstitute existing stone/soil without creating new matter.

**Closest canon analogues:**
- **Memory Erasure** — the only other confirmed glaive-using spell; shares the depth-penetration mechanic but targets the mind rather than biological tissue.
- **Scalewolf Curse** — another forbidden body-transformation spell; transforms the body into a creature form rather than a slime state; may share structural similarities.
- **Petrification** — forbidden area-body-transformation that converts tissue to stone; the Slime Rendering Seal is the fluid counterpart (tissue to slime) rather than the rigid one.

**Bottom line:** The Slime Rendering Seal is a glaive-anchored forbidden body-transformation spell. The glaive confirmation is its only concrete mechanistic detail. Everything else — the sigil, the specific transformation mechanism, the sign arrangement — is unknown. No defensible recipe exists.
