# Scalewolf Curse

> A forbidden transformation spell that permanently fuses a person with creature material — in the documented case, it fused Euini with his scalewolf-fur-and-scale Mirror Cloak, turning him into a scalewolf.

## Overview
The Scalewolf Curse is a forbidden body-affecting spell used by the Brimmed Cap witch Sasaran in the Serpentback Cave arc. When cast on Euini, it caused his Mirror Cloak of Borrowshade (which contained scalewolf fur and scales) to physically fuse with his body, transforming him permanently into a scalewolf. The seal shares one central sigil with the Illusory Labyrinth — described as "three dots arranged in a triangle" — which has been identified as the **scalewolf decorative sigil**. All other sigils and signs within the seal are unknown to the analysts at time of writing.

## In plain terms (sum up)
The Scalewolf Curse is essentially a permanent and forbidden body-fusion spell: it merges a person with whatever creature material is on or near them. Euini was wearing a cloak made from scalewolf parts; the spell fused those parts into him and transformed him. Inverting the seal's signs restores the mind but not the body — full reversal requires a completely different spell (the Anti Scalewolf Curse). Think of it as a magical bonding agent that locks creature-identity onto a human body.

## Deduced effect
The seal's canon effect is confirmed: it permanently transforms the target into a scalewolf by fusing creature material present on or near them. The scalewolf sigil's presence explains the **type** of transformation — it imposes scalewolf-identity onto the substance it acts on, just as decorative sigils impose a creature-shape. The body becomes the substrate; the creature material (fur, scales) becomes the substance that is merged.

Because the full sign set is unknown, the mechanistic pathway is speculative. Four ranked hypotheses:

1. **Most likely — convergence / bind + creature-identity imposition:** The seal uses a `convergence`-class or `bind` sign to force the creature substance (cloak material) to converge into and lock onto the human body. The scalewolf sigil supplies the creature-shape template; the binding sign fuses it permanently. This parallels how convergence compacts loose particles into rigidity in earth spells — here the "particles" are creature-components being fused into living tissue.

2. **Possible — weave / entwine applied to creature material:** A `weave` or `entwine` sign could cause the cloak (a fabric/material object) to interweave with the body on the cellular level. The scalewolf sigil would template the resulting form. This would explain why the Mirror Cloak's presence was the catalyst — the spell needed a physical "ribbon" of creature material to weave with.

3. **Possible — region + scalewolf sigil for body-space redefinition:** One or more `region` signs could define the body as the target domain for the scalewolf substance to manifest within. The Illusory Labyrinth (which shares the sigil) uses column + region + dispersion to distort space — a similar spatial-redefinition mechanic could be redirected at a human body to distort its form.

4. **Long-shot — time / stop sigil component:** The Petrification spell (another forbidden body spell from the same magic book) incorporates a time-stop sub-seal. If the Scalewolf Curse similarly uses a time-like component, it could lock the body at the transformed state, preventing natural reversion. This would explain why simple inversion only partially reverses the curse (restoring the mind, a time-reversible state, but not the physical transformation, which is "stopped" in place).

*All hypotheses are speculation; none are confirmed by canon.*

## Element behavior & canon grounding
Decorative sigils impose a **shape or identity** onto the substance the spell operates on (CORE.md §7; confirmed by World Guide 2026). The scalewolf sigil is a creature-decorative glyph — it defines the creature-form that the spell expresses. In most spells, decorative sigils are largely aesthetic (a horse-shaped water construct, a dragon-shaped water flow). The Scalewolf Curse goes further: it imposes creature-identity onto a **living human body**, which is the exact class of action forbidden by the Day of the Pact ([forbidden-magic.md:7](../forbidden-magic.md)).

The distinguishing feature of this curse over ordinary transformations is that it leverages **existing creature material** (the Mirror Cloak). The cloak's scalewolf-derived materials serve as the feedstock the spell manipulates — consistent with the broader pattern that body-altering forbidden magic works *through* substances already in contact with the body rather than conjuring matter from nothing.

**Closest canon analogue:** The Illusory Labyrinth shares the scalewolf sigil at its core. But that spell's column/region/dispersion signs redirect the sigil's output spatially (creating a looping labyrinth) rather than merging it biologically. The Scalewolf Curse takes the same creature-identity token and points it *inward at a person* instead of outward into space.

**Bottom line:** The scalewolf sigil is the only confirmed ingredient. The full sign set that explains *how* creature-identity is fused to a human body remains unknown. Any reconstruction is hypothesis. Given that the spell is forbidden, no reproduction JSON is provided.
