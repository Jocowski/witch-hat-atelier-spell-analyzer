# Counterclock

> Reverses time on whatever it is drawn on, unwinding damage, wear, and even wounds — but the reversal lasts only up to a day before it fades and the original state reasserts itself.

## Overview
Counterclock is a family of niche-type time spells that reverse the aging and damage of objects (and, when used illegally, living bodies). Unlike the Repetition Seal, which *preserves* an object in its current state, Counterclock actively *rewinds* it to a prior state — putting a broken pot back together, restoring spilled ink, and undoing wounds. The effect is **temporary**: it lasts at most one day, then vanishes on its own, reverting the target to whatever state it was in *before* the spell was cast. The spells are made of straight lines forming many geometric shapes in a six-sided star pattern. They have no identifiable sigil (no elemental or time sigil sits at the center), which makes them distinct from — and more mysterious than — Repetition Seals. They are considered difficult to learn.

## In plain terms (sum up)
You draw Counterclock on a broken, worn, or damaged object and it winds time backward for that thing — cracks seal up, ink flows back into a pot, wounds close. This reversal holds for up to twenty-four hours. When the spell expires on its own, the clock starts ticking forward again and whatever damage was unwound comes back. The spell can be redrawn immediately to restart the effect. The most alarming use is drawing it on a person to undo mortal wounds, but this is forbidden by law — a rule every witch apparently struggles with given how obvious and powerful the temptation is. The six-sided star geometric design is quite different from a sigil-based spell: there is no elemental core, which is part of why the magic system's guardians likely classify it differently.

## Composition
- **Substance (sigils):** None identifiable. The wiki states: "no identifiable sigil" and notes they differ from Repetition Spells in this regard. The time-reversal effect suggests the substance is *time* (like the Repetition/Stop family), but there is no visible time sigil. This is the key mystery of the spell family.
- **Form (signs):** The signs are described as "straight lines which form many geometric shapes in a six-sided star shape." No individual sign names are given by the wiki. The pattern was retconned between the magazine release and Volume 14: 3 of the 6 inner triangular sections were removed in the volume version (the New Version). These geometric marks are likely the sign set that drives the reversal, but they are not currently mapped to any named sign ids.
- **Ring:** the six-sided star geometry functions as a de-facto ring structure; the spell appears to be closed/active when drawn.
- **Ink / dyes:** plain conjuring ink.

## Deduced effect
**Deduced reasoning — time-reversal without a recognized sigil.** Since the individual signs are unidentified, the full deduction is necessarily incomplete. The known facts:
1. The effect is **temporal reversal** up to 24 hours back.
2. The effect is **temporary** — not permanent; it expires after at most one day.
3. After expiry, the target returns to its pre-spell state (wounds reopen, cracks return).
4. The spell can be **redrawn** to re-extend the window.
5. The design is **six-sided star geometry** — very different from simple sigil+sign rings.

**Ranked hypotheses for the no-sigil mystery:**

1. **Most likely — unknown time sigil at center:** The center of the six-sided star may contain a time-related sigil not yet identified or not separately catalogued, analogous to how the Repetition sign was once classified as a sign and later retconned to a sigil. The reversal effect is so closely parallel to Stop/Repetition that a hidden time sigil is the simplest explanation. The six-sided star form signs around it drive the direction and extent of the reversal. *Confirmation:* close inspection of the center mark in the manga would show a distinct symbol.

2. **Possible — the geometric structure itself is the sigil:** The entire six-sided star pattern, including any central mark, forms an emergent "sigil" via its overall geometric shape — the way the Repetition sign at center *is* the core for Repetition Seals. The star-pattern acts as a time-domain substance activator by structural resonance rather than a drawn glyph. *Confirmation:* removing any single geometric piece would break the spell entirely.

3. **Possible — sign-only time magic (sigil-free pathway):** The system may permit a time-manipulation spell with no elemental sigil at all — the straight-line geometric marks are a complete sign set that collectively reverse time without needing a substance core. This would make Counterclock categorically different from elemental spells (which need a sigil). *Confirmation:* if the center of the star is truly empty (no mark), this hypothesis holds.

4. **Long-shot — compound of intermediary forms:** The wiki notes "there appear to be a number of intermediary spells between Counterclock and Repetition Seals … such as the ones on Ininia's gloves," suggesting a spectrum. Counterclock may be a highly compressed or evolved form of the Repetition family where the sigil has been absorbed into the sign geometry over generations of design. *Confirmation:* cross-reference with the Ininia's-gloves intermediate designs.

## Element behavior & canon grounding
The time substance — if it is present as a hidden core — operates on **time/state** rather than matter. The behavior is documented for the Stop and Repetition sigils: Stop halts, Repetition resets continuously. Counterclock does neither of those two things exactly — it **reverses** to a specific prior state, then holds that reversal for a finite window before releasing. This is a third time-operation: revert-once-then-hold-temporarily, rather than halt-indefinitely or reset-continuously. It needs no material source (time-type magic acts on the target's history, not its matter), and it requires a target to be drawn on.

The 24-hour cap is significant: Repetition Seals do not seem to have such a hard expiry (the Cookpot Lid runs indefinitely while the seal holds), but Counterclock does. This suggests the spell encodes a *fixed look-back window* into its geometry — the six-part star may be specifying a 24-hour time-frame through its structure.

Ininia uses a large Counterclock drawn directly on Dagda's chest to prevent him from dying — rewinds him to before he was wounded. This is explicitly **forbidden** (drawing time-affecting magic on a human body falls under the prohibition on magic drawn on or affecting the body directly). The wiki confirms: "despite the law, their nature leads every witch to think about using them on a human body" — Ininia broke the rule in an emergency.

**Closest canon analogue:**
- **Repetition Seal** — sister spell using the Repetition sigil, same time-preservation family; Repetition *holds* an object at its current state continuously, while Counterclock *reverses* damage to a prior state then releases. The intermediary spells on Ininia's gloves sit between these two endpoints.

**Bottom line:** Counterclock is the most powerful documented time-type spell in ordinary circulation. Its lack of an identifiable sigil is the deepest unresolved mystery in the spell system. The reversal effect is genuine and canon-confirmed; the exact sign mechanism is opaque. Forbidden when applied to the human body — a rule that is structurally difficult to enforce because the healing application is obvious to every witch.

## How to draw it
The exact drawing instructions cannot be given because the sign set is unidentified. What is documented:
1. The design uses **straight lines forming many geometric shapes in a six-sided star** pattern — six-fold radial symmetry.
2. The **New Version** (Volume 14+) has 3 of the 6 inner triangular sections removed compared to the magazine version.
3. Draw it directly **on the object to be rewound** (the ink pot, the wound, etc.) — it is drawn-on-target, not cast at a distance.
4. Redrawn as needed to extend the effect past the 24-hour window.
5. **Do NOT draw on a human body** — this is forbidden magic.

## Usage ideas
- Emergency repair: undo recent accidental damage to tools, containers, furniture.
- Ink/fluid recovery: reverse a spill within the window (Qifrey's canon use on the ink pot).
- Short-term preservation during transport: temporarily revert a degraded or damaged item for presentation or transit.
- The forbidden use — emergency wound reversal in a survival situation (acknowledge the law is being broken).

## Similar spells
- **Repetition Seal** — same family; Repetition preserves, Counterclock reverses.
- **Valance Leech Counterlock** — a Counterclock variant with a Valance Leech decorative sigil added to scope the target.
- **Time Stop** — Stop sigil-based total temporal halt, a related but distinct time mechanism.

## Notes & limitations
- **Forbidden on human bodies.** Drawing Counterclock on a person (even to save a life) breaks the law of magic. Ininia's use on Dagda is an illegal emergency act.
- **Temporary only.** The 24-hour limit is hard. After expiry the target returns to its pre-spell state — plan for the reversal ending, or redraw.
- **Memories are included in the reversal.** Dagda lost memories of the time affected (wiki confirms); be aware that time-reversal on a person also reverses their memories of the reverted period.
- **No identifiable sigil** — deduction is necessarily incomplete; this doc records hypotheses, not facts.
- The design was **retconned** between the magazine and Volume 14/15 (3 inner sections removed); both versions apparently function identically.
