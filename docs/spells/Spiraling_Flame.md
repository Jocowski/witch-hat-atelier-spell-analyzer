# Spiraling Flame

> A fire-type spell, used by Agott, that produces an **aimable, spiraling column of flame** — a mind-guided jet of fire that corkscrews as it travels.

## Overview
Spiraling Flame pairs a fire sigil with a column sign and the **sign of (spiraling) wind** to produce a column of *spinning* flame, then adds a **sights set** sign so the caster can aim the effect mentally. In the manga Agott casts it in the Illusory Labyrinth to shoot at the dragon pursuing the group, stalling it long enough for them to escape. It is the canonical example that reveals the sign of wind's effect: Agott names it a *"whirlwind keystone."*

## Validity
**Valid** (engine). One fire core plus five signs, radially symmetric. Only `info`-level notes:
- **Stable: radial symmetry** — the layout is balanced and neat.
- **Unbalanced projection signs: the beam skews down** — a positional artifact (both columns and the core sit in the lower half of the glyph); the spell's true aim is set by sights set, not column balance, so this "skew" is not the operative direction.

(The ring being open/closed is only the app's activation visual and is not part of the analysis.)

## Composition
- **Substance (sigils):** **Fire** (element: fire) — *"works to create and manipulate flames or heat"* ([sigils.md:13](../sigils.md#L13)). Fire creates its own material, so no fuel source is needed.
- **Form (signs):**
  - **Column ×2** (directional) — manifests the flame as a tight column/beam above the seal ([signs.md:34](../signs.md#L34)). The barrel the flame travels up.
  - **Sign of Wind ×2** (asymmetric) — the **"whirlwind keystone"**: sets the column spinning into a spiral (see Element behavior).
  - **Sights Set ×1** (directional) — aims the spell at a chosen point/target, *"mind-directed"* ([signs.md:163](../signs.md#L163)). **Upright, facing outward — not inverted** (its canon-neutral orientation points away from the seal's center).
- **Ring:** open in the source export (prepared); closing it fires the spell.
- **Ink / dyes:** plain conjuring ink, no dyes (ordinary intensity).

## Deduced effect
**Engine summary:** *"The flame is projected as a tight column or beam, skewed toward down (unbalanced signs); it is set whirling into a spinning spiral (a 'whirlwind keystone'). It is aimed at a chosen target (seemingly mind-directed)."*

**In plain terms:** the seal conjures fire (no fuel needed), the columns collimate it into a directed beam, the whirlwind keystones twist that beam into a **corkscrewing spiral**, and sights set lets the caster **aim it with their mind** — a guided, spinning lance of flame. (Discount the engine's "skewed down": that's a layout artifact, not the aim.)

### How each part shapes the spell
| Part | Role | Effect on this spell |
|------|------|----------------------|
| Fire | sigil / substance | The flame itself. Without it there is no material to burn or project. |
| Column ×2 | sign / form | Collimates the fire into a directed beam. Without it the fire blooms outward instead of projecting. |
| Sign of Wind ×2 | sign / motion (whirlwind) | Sets the column **spinning into a spiral**. Without it the beam is a straight jet (closer to Flame Shot). |
| Sights Set | sign / target | Makes the beam **aimable / mind-guided**. Without it the spell fires in a fixed direction and can't track. |

## Element behavior & canon grounding
**Fire is self-sufficient.** Unlike earth (which can only manipulate, never create, and so needs a real source of material), the fire sigil *creates* its substance ([sigils.md:13](../sigils.md#L13)). Spiraling Flame therefore conjures its own flame and needs nothing nearby to burn — the signs only shape fire that the seal itself produces.

**Why a whirlwind keystone works on fire.** Flame is gaseous and readily entrained by moving air. The Column gives the conjured fire a barrel to travel up ([signs.md:34](../signs.md#L34)); the **Sign of Wind** then wraps that flame in rotating air so it **corkscrews** up the barrel rather than running as a clean lance. This is the spell that resolves the sign of wind's otherwise-"unclear" role ([signs.md:179](../signs.md#L179)): Agott explicitly calls it a *"whirlwind keystone"* and the canon description of the spell is *"a column of spinning flame."* A spinning column also entrains surrounding air, making the burst larger and more turbulent on impact (the manga's "BWOOSH" against the dragon). *(Caveat: this whirlwind reading is firmly supported for Spiraling Flame, but the same sign serves as a plain wind sigil in the Pegasus Carriage Spell, so the spin role may not generalize.)*

**Sights set is in its canon orientation.** Sights set has never been seen inverted in canon, and its tip points **outward** ([signs.md:163](../signs.md#L163)); the earlier `inverted:true` reading was an artifact of a default "top-toward-center" placement. Upright, it does exactly what canon describes — lets the caster aim the spell at a mentally chosen target, as also seen in the Capture Pennant Spell.

**Closest canon analogue — Flame Shot Seal** (also fire + column):
- **Same:** self-created flame collimated into a directed beam — both are offensive fire projectors.
- **Differs:** Flame Shot fires a *fixed, straight* line of fire, confined and extended by Region signs; Spiraling Flame swaps that confinement for **Sign of Wind** (spin) and **Sights Set** (mind-aim).
- **Why:** the trade turns a loosed straight arrow of fire into a **guided, corkscrewing** one — steerable in flight and more turbulent on impact, at the cost of the long, focused reach a confined straight jet gives.

**Bottom line:** A no-fuel-needed fire beam that the Column shapes into a barrel, the whirlwind keystones twist into a **spinning spiral**, and Sights Set lets you **aim with your mind**. It is Flame Shot's cousin that trades fixed, long-range confinement for guidance and spin — ideal as a tracking deterrent against a fast, evasive target rather than a precise long-distance lance. Every part is canon-confirmed.

## How to draw it
- **Fire sigil** at the lower-center of the seal.
- **Two Column signs** flanking the fire near the bottom, mirror-symmetric across the vertical axis (canted slightly so the pair reads balanced — the column barrel sits above the core).
- **Two Sign of Wind** marks in the upper-left and upper-right (the curling/whirl marks) — these are what make it spiral; keep them paired for symmetry.
- **One Sights Set** at top-center, **tip facing outward** (away from the seal's center), upright and not inverted.
- Maintain bilateral (here effectively radial) symmetry for stability. A bigger/neater seal is more powerful and longer-lasting; no dyes are required.

## Usage ideas
- **Canon:** Agott fires it at the dragon in the Illusory Labyrinth as a tracking fire-burst, stalling the pursuer so the group can flee — a guided deterrent against a large, fast threat.
- **Tracking fire-lance:** the mind-aim means you don't need a clean line of sight at the moment of casting; aim mid-flight.
- **Boring/drilling jet:** the spin concentrates heat and erosion on a point, better for cutting through a barrier than a straight beam.
- **Signal flare:** a visibly corkscrewing flame is distinctive and hard to mistake.

## Similar spells
- **Flame Shot Seal** — fire + column, but a fixed straight jet confined by Region signs (no spin, no mind-aim). The nearest analogue; see above for the contrast.
- **Pyreball Seal** — fire + levitation, a floating ball of flame rather than a directed beam.
- **Capture Pennant Spell** — the other canon sights-set spell, sharing the mind-directed aiming mechanic.

## Notes & limitations
- The engine reports `spin: false` and `aim: down`; both are limitations of the geometry model, not the spell. Spin here comes from the Sign of Wind (which the engine can't measure as a tangential cant), and the "down" skew is a layout artifact — the real aim is whatever sights set targets.
- The whirlwind role of the Sign of Wind is canon-supported **for this spell**; treat it as uncertain elsewhere (it acts as a wind sigil in the Pegasus Carriage Spell).
- Not forbidden — ordinary offensive fire magic, no body/reality-warping component.
