# Petrification

> A forbidden area-effect spell that turns everything nearby — living and inanimate — into a rock-like unknown material, while apparently freezing the petrified subjects in time.

## Overview
The Petrification spell is the most dramatically consequential forbidden spell shown in Witch Hat Atelier — Coco accidentally cast it in chapter 1 by tracing it from Iguin's magic picture book, turning her house and her mother to stone. The seal contains a large number of both known and unknown elements. Confirmed components from the anime trailer and wiki: an earth sigil, column, bend, dispersion, and region ("directional signs") signs, and a **nested time-stop sub-seal** (the Stop sigil). The involvement of the earth sigil explains the stone-like result; the time-stop element likely freezes the petrified state in place, preventing natural reversal.

## In plain terms (sum up)
The Petrification spell is essentially a "turn everything to stone" spell. Whatever it touches — people, objects, the building itself — gets converted into a rigid, stone-like material. The time-stop component inside the seal explains why the petrification persists: time is halted for the affected matter, so the stone form is locked in and cannot naturally decay or change back. The spell affects a broad area rather than a single target, making it one of the most dangerous forbidden spells known. Coco's mother was still alive (Qifrey later confirms she can be restored), meaning the time-stop preserves the victims in suspended animation rather than killing them outright.

## Validity
**Partially reconstructible — several components confirmed, but a large portion of the design remains unknown.** The confirmed nucleus (earth sigil + column + bend + dispersion + region + stop sub-seal) is mechanically coherent. The remaining unknown signs are what produce the all-encompassing, large-area effect and presumably the body-affecting property that makes it forbidden.

## Deduced effect
The confirmed effect is area petrification: everything within range (living and inanimate) transforms into a rigid stone-like material. The time-stop sub-seal freezes the petrified state, preventing decay or reversion.

The earth sigil's role is the most direct: earth is a **manipulate-only** substance that cannot create matter from nothing but can alter and reshape existing matter ([sigils.md:53](../sigils.md)). For the Petrification spell, the existing matter is everything in the area — organic tissue, wood, stone, whatever is present. The earth sigil's manipulation capability, amplified and spread by the column/dispersion/region signs, converts all matter in the area to the earth-element's "stone-like" state.

Ranked hypotheses for the full mechanism:

1. **Most likely — earth as a universal material converter:** The spell forces every substance in the area through an earth-sigil "filter," converting their state to rigid stone-like material. The column/dispersion/region array spreads this conversion spatially; the bend sign shapes the spread to avoid gaps. The stop sub-seal then locks the converted state in time, preventing any reversal or further change.

2. **Possible — stop sub-seal as the primary mechanism:** The time-stop is the forbidden core — it halts time for all matter in the affected area, after which the earth element coats or replaces the halted matter. The stone-like material may not be true stone but a "time-frozen" state of the original matter that resembles stone. This would explain why Coco's mother can be restored (she is time-stopped, not destroyed) and why the result is described as "an unknown material" rather than simply "stone."

3. **Possible — nested contraption where stop feeds earth:** The stop sub-seal could be arranged so that it first halts the target matter, and then the earth element fills in/replaces the halted form. This is a two-stage contraption — the stop enables the earth manipulation by removing the target's resistance to material conversion.

4. **Long-shot — earth + region + dispersion as the "ground rising up" effect:** Rather than converting matter, the spell might cause earth to rapidly envelop everything in the area from the ground up (earth can manipulate existing stone/soil, and region + dispersion spreads the manifestation outward). The time-stop then prevents the entombed subjects from dying by freezing their biological processes.

*The precise mechanism of body-affecting conversion is not explained by the confirmed components alone.*

## Element behavior & canon grounding
Earth is a **manipulate-only** substance ([sigils.md:53](../sigils.md)) — it cannot create matter, only transform existing matter. This is actually key to understanding the Petrification spell: it does not create stone from nothing. Instead, it manipulates every material in the area, converting each substance into the earth-element's rigid stone-like state. This is the most extreme use of earth's manipulation capability — instead of reshaping a rock or moving sand, it reconstitutes *everything* as stone.

The "unknown material" description for the result is consistent with this: the spell converts matter to a stone-like state, but the original substances were varied (wood, flesh, ceramic, textile), so the resulting stone-like material has no single natural analog.

The Stop sigil in a spell is canon-confirmed as a "time stop" element. Its documented behavior in spells like Time Stop ([sigils.md]) is to freeze time for the affected subject. Nested as a sub-seal within the Petrification spell, it most naturally plays the role of locking the converted state — once earth has remade the matter as stone, stop prevents any further temporal change, making the petrification permanent until the seal is disrupted.

The column + bend + dispersion + region array is a well-documented large-area projection pattern. Dispersion "pours or disperses outwards" ([signs_explained:130]); column projects a beam; region defines the target domain; bend shapes the path. Together they make the effect omnidirectional and far-reaching — consistent with the canon observation that everything in the house and its occupants were petrified.

**Closest canon analogues:**
- **Wall Breaker Seal** — earth + column is the simplest earth-column projection; Petrification uses the same earth + column combination but with a massively expanded effect scope.
- **Time Stop** — shares the stop sigil; the standalone time-stop effect is non-forbidden; its embedded use here enables the permanent petrification.
- **Sand Cage** — earth + dispersion + crush creates a dispersing earth effect; Petrification's earth + dispersion similarly spreads the earth manipulation outward, but without crush (no pulverization — instead conversion).

**Bottom line:** Petrification is a large-area forbidden earth spell that converts all matter within range to a stone-like state, with a nested time-stop that locks in the conversion. The earth + column + dispersion + region + stop nucleus is coherent, but the body-affecting conversion and the specific "unknown material" result require additional unidentified signs. The spell is self-sufficient in energy (earth manipulates existing matter; stop halts change) but depends on existing matter as feedstock — a house full of objects and people provides ample material.

## How to draw it
**Do not draw this spell.** It is the most destructive forbidden spell shown in the manga. For reference only: confirmed components are earth sigil at the core, with column/bend/dispersion/region signs and a nested Stop sub-seal; the remaining signs are unknown.

## Usage ideas
- Area-denial and mass imprisonment (the canon implied use — forbidden for both environmental-alteration and body-affecting reasons).
- The time-stop component may be the key to reversibility — disrupting the stop mechanism before the earth conversion completes might allow partial or full restoration.

## Similar spells
- **Wall Breaker Seal** — earth + column; the simplest earth-beam spell; Petrification is this concept scaled up with spread and time-lock.
- **Sand Cage** — earth + dispersion; another dispersing earth effect; non-forbidden because it shapes rather than converts matter.
- **Time Stop** — shares the stop sigil; non-forbidden standalone.

## Notes & limitations
- **Forbidden** — directly affects the human body (converts it to stone) and counts as massive environmental destruction.
- Coco's mother survived in a preserved stone state — the time-stop element is the likely mechanism for preservation rather than death.
- The "unknown material" description from the wiki suggests the resulting stone-like substance is not ordinary earth-element stone; the conversion may be unique to this spell.
