# Illusory Labyrinth

> A forbidden environmental-distortion spell that warps space within a large underground area into an infinite loop — anyone inside walks forever without finding an exit.

## Overview
The Illusory Labyrinth is a large-scale forbidden spell used by the Brimmed Caps to trap Qifrey's apprentices in the Serpentback Cave labyrinth. The space inside becomes topologically looped: walk far enough in any direction and you return to where you started. The seal was destroyed by the apprentices (using a Serpent's Bed of Sand), after which Qifrey extracted them as the labyrinth collapsed. The spell shares its central sigil with the Scalewolf Curse — the three-dot-triangle arrangement identified as the scalewolf decorative sigil. The wiki further confirms the presence of **column signs**, **region signs ("directional signs")**, and **dispersion signs**, with remaining components unidentified.

## In plain terms (sum up)
The Illusory Labyrinth bends the space inside a large area so that it loops back on itself endlessly, like a torus. You can walk what seems like a straight path and arrive exactly where you started. It's a spatial trap, not an illusion in the sense of a false image — the space genuinely wraps. The same creature-identity sigil used in the Scalewolf Curse is at its heart, suggesting that the "scalewolf-shape" template is here applied to space rather than a body. Column, region, and dispersion signs are confirmed in the design; their arrangement likely generates and defines the extent of the distorted zone.

## Deduced effect
Confirmed: a topologically looping zone is created in a large space. Anyone inside cannot find an exit by walking — they return to the same location. The labyrinth collapses when the seal is destroyed.

The scalewolf sigil's role here is the most intriguing element: in the Scalewolf Curse it imposes creature-identity onto a body; here the same sigil appears to define the **shape-template of the zone itself** — the labyrinth is shaped by the scalewolf creature's spatial "identity," perhaps its territorial or maze-like quality. This is speculative but consistent with the decorative sigil principle: the sigil imposes a shape/identity on whatever the spell operates on.

Ranked hypotheses for the space-distortion mechanism:

1. **Most likely — region signs defining a looped manifold:** Multiple region signs arranged to point simultaneously inward and outward (or opposing each other) could create the documented "magic manifests on the ring but not inside" behavior described in the Region sign documentation, scaled up to a large area. A zone of magic that "manifests on the boundary but not the interior" with dispersion spreading it across the boundary would create a surface that is all-boundary — a topological loop.

2. **Possible — unknown signs that redraw exits as entries:** One or more unknown asymmetric or special signs may map directions in the labyrinth to other directions — a spatial-rotation or wrapping mechanic not elsewhere documented. These would explain why the known signs (column, region, dispersion) alone are insufficient.

3. **Possible — column + dispersion defining the walls + loop-back:** Column projects the effect along an axis; dispersion spreads it outward. Together they could define the "walls" of corridors that loop back on themselves, with the scalewolf creature-form templating the maze structure.

4. **Long-shot — nested contraption with repetition:** A nested seal using repetition could continuously reset the traveler's position to the labyrinth interior, preventing exit. This would be a complex contraption rather than a single-circle spell.

*The space-distortion mechanic remains unexplained by the confirmed components; the full recipe is not reconstructible.*

## Element behavior & canon grounding
The Illusory Labyrinth represents a category of forbidden magic distinct from body-transformation: **excessive environment alteration** ([forbidden-magic.md:7](../forbidden-magic.md)). Rather than acting on a person's body, it distorts a large physical space — but the effect on people trapped inside (inability to escape, effectively imprisoning them) is just as severe.

The scalewolf sigil connection to the Scalewolf Curse is the most important structural clue. CORE.md §7 states that decorative sigils "impose a shape/identity on the substance." In the Scalewolf Curse, the substance is biological tissue and the imposed identity is "scalewolf-creature-form." In the Illusory Labyrinth, the substance is **space itself** — an unusual application where the environment becomes the substrate for the creature-identity template. The resulting "labyrinthine" form matches the scalewolf creature's territorial/den characteristics (underground, complex, maze-like).

The confirmed column + region + dispersion triad parallels the combination seen in several large-area projection spells: column drives the effect along an axis, region scopes the domain, dispersion spreads it. The Water Pen uses column + dispersion for a spreading water effect; the Rising Wave uses column + region for a directionally biased water surge. The Illusory Labyrinth applies the same triad to a creature-identity substrate to define and spread a spatial-warp zone.

**Closest canon analogue:** The Windowway Spell creates a spatial portal between two locations — a different but related manipulation of space. That spell allows passage between fixed points; the Illusory Labyrinth corrupts *all* spatial passage within a domain. Both represent forbidden-tier environmental manipulation that reshapes how space functions for anyone within or using it.

**Bottom line:** The Illusory Labyrinth is a large-scale environmental forbidden spell. The confirmed components (scalewolf sigil, column, region, dispersion) establish it as a creature-identity-templated spatial projection, but the topological loop mechanic requires additional unidentified signs. No complete reproduction is possible.
