# Twin Bottles' Spell

> A forbidden space-type spell drawn on a pair of matching objects that links their liquid contents across any distance, keeping the fluid in each bottle perfectly synchronized regardless of separation.

## Overview
The Twin Bottles' Spell (also called the Twinned Bottles' Spell) is a forbidden space-linking spell with a long history: before the Day of the Pact, healing witches used it on paired bottles to share tonics — if one healer's bottle was depleted, the other's supply would compensate, keeping contents evenly split regardless of distance. After the Pact, the spell was forbidden. Iguin exploited it in the main story by hiding tiny glass pieces inscribed with the spell inside ink bottles — one kept by himself, one secretly placed in Coco's supplies — to remotely deliver forbidden blood-ink to her without her knowledge. The confirmed signs are **two convergence signs** and **two region signs** both pointing toward the center; all other signs are unidentified. The spell functions as a spatial link between two physical objects.

## In plain terms (sum up)
The Twin Bottles' Spell is a magical synchronization link between two containers. Whatever liquid is in one bottle is mirrored in the other, and the two are kept evenly split no matter how far apart they are. It's the magical equivalent of two communicating vessels, except it works across any distance. The spell is forbidden because it manipulates matter across space in a way that couldn't be regulated after the Day of the Pact. Iguin used it as a secret delivery system — by remotely filling Coco's ink bottle with his own forbidden blood-ink without physically going near her.

## Deduced effect
Confirmed: the spell links two containers' fluid contents across any distance, keeping the total amount evenly split between them. When Iguin filled his bottle, the corresponding amount appeared in Coco's bottle. The link is bidirectional and passive (it maintains the balance continuously).

Ranked hypotheses for the synchronization mechanism:

1. **Most likely — convergence as the teleportation anchor:** The two convergence signs on each spell instance function as "convergence points" that are magically paired. Fluid converging to the center of one bottle is simultaneously drawn to the center of the paired bottle — the convergence signs are not just focusing one object but establishing a shared spatial nexus across distance. The region signs define "inside this container" as the domain that feeds this anchor.

2. **Possible — mirror/window mechanic with region signs:** The two region signs pointing inward could define a "spatial window" connecting the interior volumes of the two bottles. Whatever is inside one spatial domain is simultaneously inside the other. The convergence signs maintain the equal-split balance by continuously drawing the fluid to the center of each defined volume. This would make it a small-scale Windowway Spell variant focused on fluid dynamics.

3. **Possible — guidance-based attraction across instances:** The spell may involve a guidance-class sigil (not yet identified) that attracts the "same" substance across the two bottles' instances. When one bottle's fluid level changes, the guidance pulls the balance from the other. The convergence signs would serve as the intake mechanism at each end.

4. **Long-shot — link sign as the cross-distance connector:** The `link` sign is documented as part of the magic system's contraption vocabulary. A pair of spells with link signs could be the actual bridge between the two bottles, with convergence and region handling the fluid dynamics at each end. This would make the Twin Bottles' Spell a permanent paired contraption rather than a single-circle spell.

*All hypotheses are speculative; the cross-distance mechanic is not explained by the confirmed signs alone.*

## Element behavior & canon grounding
The Twin Bottles' Spell operates in a category the standard CORE.md substance model does not fully explain: **spatial linking across distance**. Ordinary spells operate on substances at the seal's location. A cross-distance synchronization spell must somehow connect two distant loci, which is a different kind of magic than element manipulation.

Convergence is documented as making magic "converge, centering down to a single point" and causing "loose particles to pack tightly together" ([signs_explained:184]). In a paired spell, two convergence signs — one on each bottle — could define a shared spatial "center" that exists between them, making the two bottles' interiors topologically adjacent at that point. This is unusual but consistent with the Windowway Spell precedent, which also creates a spatial adjacency between two physically separate locations.

Region signs pointing inward define the "inside" of the container as the active zone. Two region signs directed inward strongly suggests both the interior volume is the domain and that the spell's action is wholly contained within the bottle — visible externally as the liquid moving, but operating invisibly at the spatial level.

The historical healing-witch use is key context: the spell maintained equal distribution of tonics "regardless of distance." This long-range, persistent, passive-balance property is the defining functional feature. It makes the spell economically valuable (no need for a courier; share a vital medicine remotely) and militarily/covertly dangerous (Iguin's use: deliver forbidden substances without physical presence).

**Closest canon analogue:** The **Windowway Spell** is the nearest spatial-manipulation spell — it creates a portal between two fixed points. The Twin Bottles' Spell is a smaller-scale, fluid-specific version of the same spatial-linking principle, applied to the interior volumes of two physical containers. It is also comparable to the **Pouch of Calling Seal**, which uses the Calling sigil to link a physical container to a stored recording — a different kind of non-spatial link between two physical objects.

**Bottom line:** The confirmed nucleus (two convergence + two region/inward) is a plausible spatial-focus framework, but the cross-distance mechanic requires additional unidentified signs. The spell is forbidden due to spatial manipulation beyond regulated use, and was exploited for covert forbidden-substance delivery.
