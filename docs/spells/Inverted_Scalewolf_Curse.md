# Inverted Scalewolf Curse

> A niche-type spell derived by inverting the Scalewolf Curse; it restores the mind of someone transformed by the curse, but cannot reverse the physical transformation.

## Overview
The Inverted Scalewolf Curse was devised collaboratively by Agott, Coco, and Tetia while trapped in the Serpentback Cave, as an improvised attempt to help Euini after Sasaran's Scalewolf Curse transformed him. The concept was straightforward: inversion reverses an invertible spell's effect. They predicted this would undo the transformation. Agott drew and applied it directly onto the transformed Euini. The result was a partial success — Euini's mind and personality were restored, but his body remained in scalewolf form. The spell is classified **niche** (not forbidden), because as noted by Tetia and Agott in the manga, it technically only undoes an effect — it has not been stated to be forbidden in itself. Notably, despite supposedly being the same signs inverted, the two seals do not look visually similar, which may indicate the full composition of the Scalewolf Curse was not known and the apprentices' reconstruction was imprecise.

## In plain terms (sum up)
Agott and the other apprentices figured out that if you flip a spell's signs upside-down (invert them), it often reverses the spell's effect — like how inverting the Crush sign causes it to *reassemble* things instead of smash them. They tried this with the Scalewolf Curse to cure Euini. It worked on his mind — he regained his thoughts and personality — but his body stayed transformed. The physical fusion was too deep for inversion alone to undo. A completely different spell (the Anti Scalewolf Curse) was needed for that. Euini later used the Anti Scalewolf Curse collar to toggle between human and scalewolf form, but kept the Inverted Scalewolf Curse's legacy as proof the apprentices' understanding of inversion was mechanically correct.

## Deduced effect
The partial reversal result is canon-confirmed. Two layers of explanation:

1. **Mind restoration:** The creature-identity imposed by the Scalewolf Curse acts on two levels — a mental/behavioral "shapeshifting of self" and a physical "fusing of substance." Inversion successfully reverses the mental layer because it is an operator-reversible state (like time-reversal or crush/reassemble — running the sign backwards reasserts the prior identity). The mind is an "immaterial" substance in the CORE.md model's terms, subject to the same logic as a form being restored.

2. **Physical non-reversal:** The physical fusion of scalewolf material into the body is a **substance-level change**, not just an operator-level change. The creature material was physically bonded to living tissue. Inverting the operators that caused the bonding does not unbond the material already merged — it would need a separate mechanism to remove or expel the substance. This is why the Anti Scalewolf Curse, which acts on the substance rather than the operator direction, can achieve what inversion cannot.

The apprentices' reconstruction being visually dissimilar to the Scalewolf Curse may indicate they did not know the original's full sign set and produced a best-guess inversion.

## Element behavior & canon grounding
Inversion is a documented mechanic: "if inverted (facing outwards), the effect of the sign will be the opposite of what it is usually" ([signs_explained:107](../signs.md#L107)). The Crush / Integration pair is the canonical example: Crush breaks apart, inverted Crush reassembles. The apprentices correctly applied this logic.

The failure case illuminates a limit of the inversion mechanic: inversion reverses *operator direction*, but it cannot reconstitute *missing substance*. After the Scalewolf Curse, the physical substance (the scalewolf material) is merged into Euini's body at the material level — there is now *more substance* in the body than there was before, fused together. Inverting the operator does not remove that extra matter. To do so requires an expulsion or purification operation, which is exactly what the Anti Scalewolf Curse (a different spell) provides.

**Closest canon analogue:** The Wall Breaker / Integration pair is the closest documented example of inversion at work. Wall Breaker (Crush, inward) pulverizes; Integration (Crush, outward/inverted) reassembles powder into the original form. This analogy predicts the Inverted Scalewolf Curse should fully reverse the transformation — which it nearly does. The gap between "nearly" and "completely" is the physical substance-bonding that inversion cannot address.

**Bottom line:** The Inverted Scalewolf Curse is a correctly-principled application of sign inversion by the apprentices, and it works on the mental/behavioral layer of the Scalewolf Curse. Its limitation is inherent to the inversion mechanic itself: it reverses operators, not substance-presence. Since the full sign set is unknown, no reproduction JSON is provided.
