# Lockwax

> A niche sealing spell stamped in wax over a scroll to bind it shut and protect its contents — not commonly used, reserved for powerful or important documents.

## Overview
Lockwax is a rare niche spell used to seal scrolls against unauthorized opening. The seal is pressed into the wax closure of a scroll (like a wax seal ring), and it adds a magical lock to the binding. Qifrey's surprised reaction upon seeing it (ch. 50) implies it is unusual — either uncommon in practice or reserved for very sensitive material. The scroll sealed with Lockwax was eventually used by Qifrey in his battle against Engendale (ch. 69).

## In plain terms (sum up)
Lockwax is a magical wax seal for scrolls — like a normal wax signet ring stamp, but with a spell embedded in it. Break the wax and the spell is broken; keep it intact and the scroll stays locked. It's the witch world's version of tamper-evident security for important documents. Qifrey's surprise reaction suggests it's an unusual or prestigious choice rather than a routine everyday seal. The exact mechanism (how it locks, whether it alarms, whether it destroys the contents if tampered with) is not confirmed by canon.

## Composition
- **Substance (sigils):** An unidentified sigil in the inner circle. Its nature is entirely unknown — this is the biggest gap in the recipe.
- **Form (signs):**
  - **Arrow-like sign with triangle on end, pointing toward center (×3, one per section):** The "arrow with triangle tip pointing inward" matches the description of the **Pull** sign (`pull` — a vertical line ending in a large double-chevron arrowhead, pointing inward = draws matter toward the glyph) or possibly the **Region/direction** sign (`direction` — a chevron/caret with apex pointing inward). The triangular tip is more consistent with Pull's "large double-chevron arrowhead" than Region's simple caret. Pull pointing inward = attract/draw in, which in a locking context could mean "draw the scroll's closure tight" or "bind the wax to the paper."
  - **Two short lines per section (×3 sets = 6 total), one bending at right angle:** This shape — two lines, one bending 90° over the other — could be: (a) the `bind` sign (if bind is a rectangular bracket shape); (b) `unknown_07` (two parallel horizontal bars, an "=" shape) with an angled variant; or (c) an uncatalogued mark. Given the right-angle bend description, `bind` (which constrains/locks targets) is the most semantically appropriate candidate. However, this is unconfirmed.
- **Structural note:** Three identical sections at 120° intervals gives the seal **3-fold radial symmetry**.
- **Ring:** Closed.
- **Ink / dyes:** Applied in wax — the spell is stamped rather than drawn; this is a pre-prepared seal. The wax medium is not standard conjuring ink; whether this affects the spell's mechanics is unknown.

## Deduced effect

If the signs are Pull (inward, ×3) + a binding sign (×6), the effect would be: **three inward-pulling forces drawing the scroll's closure in from equally spaced directions, combined with binding marks that lock those forces in place**. The three-point symmetry suggests the pull is radially balanced (like three equally-spaced clamps) — not aiming in one direction but converging from all thirds simultaneously. This reads as a **magical cinch or lock**: the wax is pulled in from three sides and bound shut.

**Ranked hypotheses for the mechanism:**

1. *Most likely — Earth/stop binding (moderate confidence):* the `stop` sigil ("halts things in their path, refuses to let them move") could provide the substance — "halt the opening." Combined with inward Pull and bind signs, the spell physically resists the scroll being opened: the stop substance locks the closure's state, and the Pull/bind marks enforce it on the wax material.

2. *Possible — Guidance attraction (low-moderate confidence):* a `guidance` sigil creating attraction between the scroll's paper and its closure, making them cling together. Pull signs would reinforce this attraction. Breaking the wax seal would disrupt the guided bond.

3. *Possible — Unknown specialized sigil (low confidence):* a sigil specific to sealing/locking that doesn't map to any current id (similar to how Memory Erasure uses a specialized "time-of-memory" substance). The wiki notes Lockwax is "not commonly used" — it may use a rare or specialized sigil.

4. *Long-shot — Crystal (very low confidence):* crystal is `rigid` and can `create` its own material; a crystalline grip could hold scroll edges shut. But canon crystal spells tend toward visible, physical constructions, and Lockwax appears invisible once the wax dries.

**What would confirm each hypothesis:** seeing the inner sigil clearly in a manga panel, or a second spell using the same sigil, would resolve the substance.

## Element behavior & canon grounding
Lockwax is applied as a **wax stamp** rather than drawn in standard conjuring ink. This is mechanically notable: most spells are drawn fresh in conjuring ink on a surface. A pre-stamped wax seal is more like a **pre-cast spell preserved in the wax medium** — the seal is ready to activate when the wax is pressed. Whether wax as a medium changes the magic's behavior (permanence, activation condition, triggering on breakage) is not specified in the wiki.

The **three-section radial layout** is unusual — most canon spells use 4-fold or 8-fold symmetry. Three-fold symmetry creates a more compact, stable arrangement with fewer signs, which is consistent with a small wax stamp (limited drawing area).

**Closest canon analogue:** **Valance Leech Counterlock** — another locking/sealing spell (specifically the lock that restrains the Valance Leech on a scroll). Both are scroll-protection mechanisms. The Counterlock uses `bind` and `link` signs to enforce restraint; if the right-angle short-line marks in Lockwax are also `bind`, then Lockwax and Counterlock share the same binding grammar.

**Bottom line:** Lockwax is a scroll-protection seal that uses inward-pulling signs and binding marks in a three-fold symmetric layout to cinch a scroll's closure shut. The substance (inner sigil) is unidentified, preventing full deduction. The most likely mechanism is some form of "halt + pull + bind" on the scroll's wax medium. It is rare — suggesting either a complex recipe or an unusual sigil not in everyday use.

## How to draw it
1. Draw the **unknown inner sigil** at the center (identity unknown).
2. Divide the seal into **three equal sections** (at 120° intervals).
3. In each section, draw one **inward-pointing arrow-with-triangle** sign (Pull or Region) aimed toward the center.
4. Beside each arrow, draw **two short lines** (one bending at 90°) — the binding/clamping marks.
5. Close the ring.
6. The spell is typically stamped as a **wax seal** rather than drawn fresh — the composition is prepared in advance and pressed onto the scroll closure.

## Usage ideas

- **Secure container sealing:** the same stamp could be applied to boxes, pouches, or other closures, not just scrolls.
