# Forbidden Flames

> A spell of unknown classification that creates a small fire; its forbidden status is uncertain, as the wiki notes it "burns like a normal fire spell" and no confirmed forbidden properties have been identified.

## Overview
Forbidden Flames is one of the spells Coco copied from Iguin's magic picture book in chapter 1, alongside Petrification and others. It produces a small fire and, superficially, appears similar to ordinary fire spells. The spell uses column signs, one unidentified sign, and an unidentified sigil — the sigil is explicitly stated to be unknown (not the standard fire sigil). Whether the spell is actually forbidden in a meaningful sense is ambiguous: the wiki states it "burns like a normal fire spell" and notes it "is unknown if it has any forbidden properties or not." Its presence in Iguin's book of forbidden spells is the primary evidence it may be something more than a basic flame.

## In plain terms (sum up)
Forbidden Flames creates a small fire. It looks like an ordinary fire spell from the outside. The strange part is its sigil — it is not the standard fire sigil, meaning the fire it creates comes from a different magical source than normal. What makes it potentially "forbidden" is unclear: it could be a fire spell built with a specially powerful or dangerous sigil, it could use an element that creates fire through a forbidden mechanism (affecting living material, an uncontrolled element), or it could be a mislabeled entry in Iguin's book. Its unknown sigil is the key mystery.

## Deduced effect
Confirmed effect: a small fire, burning similarly to a normal fire spell. The column signs project the fire in a directed beam or column form.

Ranked hypotheses for the unknown sigil and its potential "forbidden" quality:

1. **Most likely — an alternate fire-family sigil (unburning flame or smoke):** The unknown sigil could be `unburning_flame` (a fire-family sigil in the catalog that produces heatless or unnatural flame) or `smoke` (which produces smoke rather than fire). Either would explain why the output "burns like a normal fire spell" visually but behaves differently at a physics level. If the flame does not produce heat, it might be more dangerous in specific contexts (cannot be extinguished normally).

2. **Possible — a sigil representing an elemental combination that is forbidden by nature:** The sigil might represent a substance that can produce fire but which has dangerous secondary properties not visible at the surface level — for example, it might deplete air in an enclosed space, or produce an invisible toxic component.

3. **Possible — the "forbidden" label is Iguin's framing, not a true classification:** Iguin sold the book to a non-witch child and included this spell among the dangerous ones. If the book was designed to lure non-witch children into drawing forbidden magic, Iguin may have included some ordinary-ish spells to make the book seem accessible and teach basic magic to the unwitting. The "forbidden" label might be Iguin's characterization, not the Pact's official classification.

4. **Long-shot — a sigil for fire that creates fire from biological sources:** Some pre-Pact fire magic may have sourced its fuel from living matter. A sigil that creates fire by consuming nearby organic material would be forbidden under both the body-affecting and environmental-destruction rules, while still "burning like a normal fire spell" visually.

## Element behavior & canon grounding
If the unknown sigil were the standard `fire` sigil, the spell would be an ordinary fire-beam (fire + column) comparable to the Flame Shot Seal. The fact that it explicitly uses a different sigil while producing fire-like output indicates one of two things: either the sigil is a fire-adjacent element (unburning flame, smoke) that produces fire-like output through a different mechanism, or the sigil is something entirely novel whose fire-output is a secondary effect.

Column is a well-understood sign: it gives the fire a directed form. Whatever the sigil produces, column shapes that output into a beam or jet. The second unknown sign modifies this further in a way that cannot be reasoned without identification.

The wiki's categorization of this spell under "Unknown Seals" (not "Forbidden Seals" in the nav bar, despite the name and context) supports the hypothesis that its forbidden status is ambiguous. The Petrification, Scalewolf Curse, Illusory Labyrinth, and other clearly forbidden spells appear under "Forbidden Seals" in the navigation — Forbidden Flames does not.

**Closest canon analogues:**
- **Flame Shot Seal** — fire sigil + column; the standard fire-beam spell. If Forbidden Flames uses a different sigil, it diverges here.
- **Phantasmal Fireball** — another fire-type in Iguin's arsenal; uses unburning flame sigil for a heatless fireball. If Forbidden Flames' sigil is `unburning_flame`, it would be the column-projection cousin of Phantasmal Fireball.
- **Pyreball Seal** — fire + levitation; the standard fire-levitation spell for comparison.

**Bottom line:** Forbidden Flames is a small-fire spell with an unidentified sigil and one unidentified sign alongside confirmed column signs. Its forbidden status is genuinely uncertain. The unknown sigil is the critical missing piece — without it, no meaningful distinction from an ordinary fire spell can be made. The most likely scenario is a fire-adjacent sigil (unburning flame or smoke) producing unusual fire properties; the "forbidden" label may reflect Iguin's intentional framing rather than a Pact violation.
